# Equities

An **equity index** is a single number that follows the prices of a group of company shares. When most of
those shares go up, the index goes up. The S&P 500, for example, follows 500 large US companies. This app
has three simulated indices: INDEX_US, INDEX_TECH and INDEX_EU.

When you **buy** an index (a *long* position), you make money if its price rises. When you **sell** it
(a *short* position), you make money if its price falls. Traders buy when they expect a rise and sell when
they expect a fall.

The **P&L** (profit and loss) shows how much a trade has gained or lost so far:

- BUY: P&L = (price today − trade price) × units
- SELL: P&L = (trade price − price today) × units

For example, on the Trade desk you bought 100 units of INDEX_US on 3 March 2025 at 5,159.73. On
18 December 2025 the index is at 4,665.11, so the P&L is (4,665.11 − 5,159.73) × 100 = **−$49,462**: a loss,
because the price fell. Had you sold instead, the same move would have given **+$49,462**.

## Quiz

Q: You BUY an index and its price falls. What happens to your P&L?
A) It is positive
B) It is negative
C) It does not change
Answer: B
Why: A buyer gains when the price rises, so a falling price means a loss.

Q: You SELL 10 units at 100. Today the price is 90. What is your P&L?
A) −$100
B) $0
C) +$100
Answer: C
Why: For a sell, P&L = (trade price − price today) × units = (100 − 90) × 10 = +$100.

Q: What does an equity index measure?
A) The price of one company's share
B) The prices of a group of company shares, as one number
C) The interest rate on government bonds
Answer: B
Why: An index combines the prices of many shares into one number that shows how that group is doing.
