# Bonds

A **bond** is a loan. When you buy a government bond, you lend money to the government. In return, it pays
you a fixed **coupon** every year and gives back the **face value** (100) at **maturity**. The app has five
government bonds, GOV_1Y, GOV_2Y, GOV_5Y, GOV_10Y and GOV_30Y, maturing in 1 to 30 years.

The **yield** is the return the market demands today for lending for that long. A bond's **price** is the
value today of all its future payments, discounted at that yield:

Price = coupon ÷ (1 + yield) + coupon ÷ (1 + yield)² + … + (coupon + 100) ÷ (1 + yield)ⁿ

Price and yield move in **opposite directions**. If yields rise, new bonds pay more, so an old bond with a
lower coupon is worth less and its price falls. If yields fall, its price rises. When the coupon equals the
yield, the price is exactly 100 ("at par"): a 10-year 4% bond costs 100 at a 4% yield, 92.28 at 5% and
108.53 at 3%.

The **yield curve** shows the yields of all maturities on one day. You can see it for any date in 2025 on
the Market page.

For example, on the Trade desk, buying 1,000,000 of GOV_10Y on 3 March 2025 gives a 4.50% coupon (the
market yield of 4.40%, rounded to the nearest 0.25%) at a price of 100.756. By 18 December 2025 the 10-year
yield had fallen to 3.95%, so the bond's price rose and the P&L is **+$37,278**.

## Quiz

Q: Yields rise. What happens to the price of a bond you already hold?
A) It rises
B) It falls
C) It stays the same
Answer: B
Why: Price and yield move in opposite directions: new bonds now pay more, so the old bond is worth less.

Q: A 10-year bond pays a 4% coupon and the market yield is 4%. What is its price?
A) 92.28
B) 100
C) 108.53
Answer: B
Why: When the coupon equals the yield, the bond is priced at par: 100.

Q: What is a bond's coupon?
A) The fixed interest it pays every year
B) The price you pay to buy it
C) The date it matures
Answer: A
Why: The coupon is the yearly interest; the face value of 100 is paid back at maturity.
