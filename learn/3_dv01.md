# Rate risk (DV01)

Yields move every day, so the value of every bond position moves with them. Traders measure this risk in
**basis points** (bp): 1 bp = 0.01%. A yield moving from 4.00% to 4.01% has risen by 1 bp.

**DV01** ("dollar value of one basis point") is how much a position's value changes when yields rise by
1 bp. For a bond you **bought**, the value falls by its DV01 when yields rise by 1 bp, and rises by its DV01
when they fall by 1 bp. For a bond you **sold**, the DV01 is negative: the position gains when yields rise.

The longer the maturity, the bigger the DV01, because more of the payments are far in the future and their
discounted value reacts more to the yield. For 1,000,000 of a 4% bond at a 4% yield:

- 1 year: $96 per bp
- 10 years: $811 per bp
- 30 years: $1,727 per bp

For example, the GOV_10Y trade from the Bonds tab (1,000,000 bought on 3 March 2025) has a DV01 of
**$835** on 18 December 2025. If the 10-year yield rises by 10 bp, the position loses about 10 × $835 =
**$8,350**. If the same bond had been sold, its DV01 would be **−$835**, and that rise would be a gain.

Adding up the DV01 of every bond gives the risk of the whole book. The Portfolio page shows the DV01 by bond,
so a risk manager can see at a glance which maturity carries the most rate risk.

## Quiz

Q: What is 1 basis point?
A) 1%
B) 0.1%
C) 0.01%
Answer: C
Why: A basis point is one hundredth of a percent: 0.01%, or 0.0001.

Q: A position has a DV01 of $500. Yields rise by 4 bp. What happens to its value?
A) It falls by about $2,000
B) It rises by about $2,000
C) It falls by about $500
Answer: A
Why: The value falls by the DV01 for each basis point: 4 × $500 = $2,000.

Q: Which bond has the biggest DV01 for the same amount?
A) The 1-year bond
B) The 10-year bond
C) The 30-year bond
Answer: C
Why: The longer the maturity, the more the price reacts to a change in yield.
